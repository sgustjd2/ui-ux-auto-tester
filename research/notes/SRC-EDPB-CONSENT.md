## Scope and applicability

- Subject: the notion of consent as a lawful basis under the GDPR. It covers the definition in Art. 4(11), the conditions in Art. 7, children in Art. 8, explicit consent in Arts. 9, 22, and 49(1)(a), and how consent interacts with other lawful bases. The EDPB adopted it with regard to Art. 70(1)(e) GDPR, the EEA Agreement, and its Rules of Procedure (preamble).
- Jurisdiction: EU/EEA. Footnote 1 says references to "Member States" are to be read as EEA Member States.
- Who it addresses: controllers that rely on consent. It covers any channel (web, app, IoT interface, email, phone, paper) and is technology-neutral.
- ePrivacy link (paras 6–7, fn 27): the EDPB states that the GDPR conditions for valid consent apply in situations within the scope of the ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC, which covers cookies and online tracking. This is what connects the guidance to cookie banners and cookie walls. The guidelines do not decide which cookies or trackers need consent; that is an ePrivacy question outside this document.
- Force: the document does not state its own legal force. It is interpretive guidance. The binding obligations are in the GDPR (SRC-EU-GDPR, VERIFIED 2026-09-23). Audit rules from this source are therefore not LEGAL on their own (tier policy, `docs/standards-research-plan.md` §3).

## Structure

Sections as printed in the table of contents (p. 3):

- 0 Preface (unnumbered paragraphs). Explains the update from WP259. Only two issues were revised: the validity of consent obtained through "cookie walls" (Conditionality, paras 38–41) and Example 16 on scrolling (Unambiguous indication, para 86). The rest was left unchanged apart from editorial changes.
- 1 Introduction (paras 1–7). Consent is one of six lawful bases. The WP29 consent opinions (notably Opinion 15/2011, WP187) are expanded upon, not replaced. Also covers the relation to the ePrivacy Directive and draft Regulation.
- 2 Consent in Article 4(11) of the GDPR (paras 8–10)
- 3 Elements of valid consent (paras 11–90)
  - 3.1 Free / freely given (13–54): 3.1.1 Imbalance of power (16–24), 3.1.2 Conditionality (25–41, including the cookie-wall paragraphs 39–41), 3.1.3 Granularity (42–45), 3.1.4 Detriment (46–54)
  - 3.2 Specific (55–61)
  - 3.3 Informed (62–74): 3.3.1 Minimum content requirements (64–65), 3.3.2 How to provide information (66–74)
  - 3.4 Unambiguous indication of wishes (75–90)
- 4 Obtaining explicit consent (91–102)
- 5 Additional conditions for obtaining valid consent (103–120): 5.1 Demonstrate consent (104–111), 5.2 Withdrawal of consent (112–120)
- 6 Interaction between consent and other lawful grounds in Article 6 GDPR (121–123)
- 7 Specific areas of concern in the GDPR (124–165): 7.1 Children, Article 8 (124–151, with 7.1.1 Information society service, 7.1.2 Offered directly to a child, 7.1.3 Age, 7.1.4 Children's consent and parental responsibility), 7.2 Scientific research (153–163), 7.3 Data subject's rights (164–165)
- 8 Consent obtained under Directive 95/46/EC (166–171)

Identifier scheme:

- Paragraphs are numbered continuously from 1 to 171 across sections. Paragraph 152 is printed as an empty numbered paragraph just before heading 7.2.
- Worked examples are boxed and numbered Example 1 to Example 23, plus Example 6a (the cookie wall, paras 40–41). Footnotes run 1 to 79.
- Paragraph numbers are the most precise public identifier and are what the EDPB's own April 2026 summary uses when it cites the guidelines (for example "section 3.4, paragraph 86, page 19").

Normative vs informative, in the source's own terms:

- The whole document is guidance. Within it, four kinds of text can be told apart:
  - quoted GDPR text (Art. 4(11) in para 8; Art. 7(4) and Recital 43 in fn 22; Recital 42 in para 105; Recital 32 in para 43);
  - EDPB interpretation, marked "the EDPB considers", "deems", or "is of the opinion" (for example paras 38, 64);
  - explicit best-practice recommendations (para 111 on refreshing consent; para 136 on a proportionate approach to parental consent);
  - boxed examples, which are illustrative.
- The EDPB uses "must", "cannot", and "shall" when it restates or interprets GDPR conditions, and "should" or "recommends" for good practice.

## Candidate rules

Proposed prefix `EDPBCONSENT` (see Uncertainties: the prefix `EDPB` is already registered to SRC-EDPB-DECEPTIVE). Identifier = paragraph number, with a `-n` suffix when one paragraph yields more than one requirement.

Proposed rule class for all rules: BEST_PRACTICE (T2 guidance cannot produce LEGAL). Each rule gets a `related_rules` link to the matching GDPR LEGAL rule once SRC-EU-GDPR is verified. Normative strength follows the EDPB's own wording, pending the lead's decision (Uncertainties). Category: `trust_privacy`, subcategory from `prd.md` §10.11. Jurisdiction: EU.

Testability is written as automated / visual / manual.

Freely given (3.1):

- EDPBCONSENT-39 — §3.1.2 paras 39–41, Example 6a. Access to a service or its content must not depend on agreeing to storage of, or access to, information on the user's device (a cookie wall). Blocking content until "Accept cookies" is clicked means consent is not freely given. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST. PARTIAL (overlay blocks content and no refuse path exists) / FULL / FULL. Subcategory consent-clarity (also obstruction).
- EDPBCONSENT-26 — §3.1.2 paras 26–28, 32–33, Examples 1 and 6; also paras 13 and 15. Providing a service must not depend on consent to processing that is not necessary for that service (tying or bundling). If it does, consent is presumed not free. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST. NONE / PARTIAL / PARTIAL (necessity needs judgment).
- EDPBCONSENT-37 — §3.1.2 paras 37–38. Offering, from the same controller, a genuinely equivalent service without the additional consent removes the conditionality. An alternative offered only by a different controller does not. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST (condition). NONE / NONE / PARTIAL.
- EDPBCONSENT-42 — §3.1.3 paras 42–45, Example 7, with §3.2 para 60. Where there are several purposes, offer a separate opt-in per purpose rather than one bundled consent. BEST_PRACTICE, SHOULD (para 60 wording; Example 7 calls non-granular consent invalid). PARTIAL (compare the number of controls with the purposes listed) / FULL / FULL. Subcategory consent-clarity.
- EDPBCONSENT-46 — §3.1.4 paras 46–49, Examples 8–9. Refusing or withdrawing consent must be possible without detriment: no cost and no downgraded service. Losing only a permissible incentive is not detriment. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST. NONE / PARTIAL / PARTIAL (withdraw, then compare features).
- EDPBCONSENT-24 — §3.1.1 para 24, §3.1.4 para 47. The consent flow must involve no deception, intimidation, coercion, or significant negative consequence, such as substantial extra cost. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST. NONE / PARTIAL / FULL. Overlaps SRC-EDPB-DECEPTIVE.

Specific (3.2):

- EDPBCONSENT-55 — §3.2 paras 55–57, fn 30. Each consent names a specific purpose. Vague purposes do not meet "specific" without more detail. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST. NONE / PARTIAL / FULL. Subcategory consent-clarity.
- EDPBCONSENT-61 — §3.2 para 55(iii) and para 61. Keep consent information clearly separate from other matters, and give information with each separate consent request about the data processed for that purpose. BEST_PRACTICE, SHOULD. PARTIAL / FULL / FULL.
- EDPBCONSENT-58 — §3.2 paras 58–59, Example 11; §3.4 para 90. A new processing purpose needs new consent. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST. NONE / NONE / PARTIAL (needs comparison over time).

Informed (3.3):

- EDPBCONSENT-64 — §3.3.1 paras 64–65. Before consent, show at least: (i) the controller's identity; (ii) the purpose of each processing operation; (iii) the type of data; (iv) that consent can be withdrawn; (v) any use for automated decision-making under Art. 22(2)(c), where relevant; (vi) transfer risks where there is no adequacy decision or Art. 46 safeguard, where relevant. Name every controller that will rely on the consent; processors need not be named for consent purposes. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST ("is required"). PARTIAL / FULL / FULL. Split per item in Phase 2. Subcategory privacy-disclosure-ux.
- EDPBCONSENT-67-1 — §3.3.2 para 67, fn 38. Use clear and plain language an average person understands. No legal jargon, and the declaration must be named as consent (fn 38 gives an "I know that…" style of wording as not clear enough). BEST_PRACTICE, SHOULD. NONE / PARTIAL / FULL.
- EDPBCONSENT-67-2 — §3.3.2 para 67. Information needed for the consent decision must not be hidden in general terms and conditions or long policies. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST ("cannot"). PARTIAL / FULL / FULL.
- EDPBCONSENT-71 — §3.3.2 paras 69, 71. An electronic consent request is clear, concise, separate, and distinct; it cannot just be a paragraph inside the terms and conditions. Layered presentation is acceptable for small screens. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST. PARTIAL / FULL / FULL.
- EDPBCONSENT-74 — §3.3.2 Example 13, fn 42. Controller identity and purpose should appear in the first layer of a layered notice. Otherwise it is hard to show consent was informed, unless the user is shown to have opened the deeper layer. BEST_PRACTICE, SHOULD (inferred from "difficult to demonstrate"). PARTIAL / FULL / FULL.
- EDPBCONSENT-70 — §3.3.2 para 70, §7.1 para 126. Fit the information to the audience; if the audience includes minors, it must be understandable to children. BEST_PRACTICE, SHOULD ("expected"). NONE / PARTIAL / FULL.
- EDPBCONSENT-73 — §3.3.2 Example 12. Testing consent wording with representative user panels is one way to show it is understandable. BEST_PRACTICE, INFORMATIVE. Method guidance for the persona layer, not an audit check.

Unambiguous indication (3.4):

- EDPBCONSENT-75 — §3.4 paras 75, 77, 84. Consent needs a deliberate, active statement or motion that can be told apart from other actions. Continuing to use a website normally is not consent. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST. PARTIAL / PARTIAL / FULL.
- EDPBCONSENT-79-1 — §3.4 paras 79, 81. Pre-ticked opt-in boxes, and opt-out constructions that need user action to prevent agreement, are invalid. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST. FULL for web DOM default checked or toggle state, PARTIAL for native apps / FULL / FULL. Subcategory preselection.
- EDPBCONSENT-79-2 — §3.4 para 79. Silence, inactivity, or simply proceeding with the service is not consent. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST. PARTIAL (see what is set before any interaction; needs an ePrivacy source to decide which cookies need consent) / NONE / FULL.
- EDPBCONSENT-81 — §3.4 para 81. Consent cannot be collected by the same action as accepting a contract or general terms; blanket acceptance of terms is not consent. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST. PARTIAL / FULL / FULL.
- EDPBCONSENT-86 — §3.4 para 86, Example 16 (revised in 05/2020). Scrolling, swiping through a page, or similar activity never counts as a clear affirmative action. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST ("will not under any circumstances"). PARTIAL (scroll, then inspect consent state) / NONE / FULL.
- EDPBCONSENT-82 — §3.4 paras 82–83, Example 15. A consent request should not interrupt the service more than necessary, though some interruption may be needed for it to work. Physical gestures can be valid if clearly explained and linked to a specific request. BEST_PRACTICE, SHOULD. NONE / PARTIAL / FULL.
- EDPBCONSENT-87 — §3.4 paras 87–89. Click fatigue weakens consent prompts and controllers are obliged to address it. Browser-setting consent must still be granular per purpose and name the controllers. BEST_PRACTICE, INFORMATIVE (heuristic input). NONE / NONE / PARTIAL.
- EDPBCONSENT-90 — §3.4 para 90. Obtain consent before the processing that needs it starts. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST. PARTIAL (network and storage evidence before interaction) / NONE / PARTIAL. Subcategory permission-timing.

Explicit consent (4):

- EDPBCONSENT-93 — §4 paras 93–98, Examples 17–18. Where explicit consent is needed (Art. 9 special-category data, Art. 22 automated decisions, Art. 49(1)(a) transfers), the user gives an express statement, for example Yes/No boxes whose text plainly states consent. Wording that only acknowledges processing is not enough. An e-signature or two-step confirmation can support this. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST. NONE / FULL / FULL.

Demonstrating consent (5.1):

- EDPBCONSENT-108 — §5.1 paras 104–108. The controller keeps evidence of when and how consent was given and what information was shown (online: session data, the consent workflow at the time, and a copy of the information shown). Pointing to a correct site configuration alone is not enough. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST. Source-code and manual review only: PARTIAL / NONE / PARTIAL.
- EDPBCONSENT-111 — §5.1 paras 110–111. Refresh consent at appropriate intervals. BEST_PRACTICE, SHOULD (the EDPB "recommends as a best practice"). NONE / NONE / PARTIAL.

Withdrawal (5.2):

- EDPBCONSENT-113 — §5.2 paras 113–114, Example 22. Withdrawal is possible at any time and as easily as consent was given. One-click consent needs equally easy withdrawal, through the same interface the consent was given in, free of charge, and without lowering service levels. Example 22 (a phone call in business hours to undo an online click) does not comply. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST. PARTIAL (find the withdrawal control on the same surface and count steps against giving consent) / FULL / FULL. Subcategory consent-clarity (also obstruction, difficult-cancellation).
- EDPBCONSENT-116 — §5.2 para 116. Tell users about the right to withdraw before they consent, and how to exercise their rights. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST. PARTIAL / FULL / FULL. Overlaps EDPBCONSENT-64 item (iv).
- EDPBCONSENT-120 — §5.2 para 120, §6 paras 122–123. After withdrawal, stop the processing. The controller may not silently switch to another lawful basis; any change must be notified. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST. NONE / NONE / PARTIAL.

Children (7.1):

- EDPBCONSENT-125 — §7.1 paras 125, 132–137, Example 23. For information society services offered directly to a child, consent below 16 (a Member State may set a lower age, not below 13) must be given or authorised by the holder of parental responsibility. Age and parental checks must be reasonable and proportionate to risk and must avoid collecting excessive data. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST. NONE / PARTIAL / FULL.
- EDPBCONSENT-149 — §7.1.4 paras 147–149. When the child reaches the age of digital consent, tell them they can withdraw consent. BEST_PRACTICE, MUST. NONE / NONE / PARTIAL.

Not proposed as UI rules:

- §3.1.1 (public-authority and employer power imbalance) is context for severity and applicability, not a UI check.
- §6, §7.2 (scientific research), §7.3, and §8 (legacy Directive 95/46/EC consents) are mostly organisational. At most they add applicability notes.

## Uncertainties and gaps

1. Canonical landing URL. Two EDPB landing pages resolve for the same document: the legacy `/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/...` path and the current `/documents/guideline/...` path. Other legacy index paths (`/our-work-tools/our-documents/publication-type/guidelines_en`, `/our-work-tools/our-documents/topic/legal-basis_en`) returned HTTP 404 on 2026-09-23, which suggests a site restructure. The current path is recorded as canonical, and the PDF URL is identical on both pages. The legacy page may stop resolving; re-check at re-verification. (OPEN_QUESTION)
2. Status inferred, not declared. EDPB landing pages carry no explicit status field. CURRENT rests on four observations: no supersession or revision notice on either landing page, the April 2026 EDPB summary pointing to these guidelines, no consent consultation open, and the version table ending at 1.1. (ASSUMPTION; re-verify per `docs/standards-research-plan.md` §5)
3. Binding anchor unverified. All GDPR article and recital content cited here is as quoted or cited by the EDPB. SRC-EU-GDPR was CANDIDATE when this note was written and is now VERIFIED (2026-09-23), so the `related_rules` links and any GDPR-level wording must be confirmed at EUR-Lex. (INCOMPLETE)
4. Rule class and strength policy for regulator guidance. The EDPB writes "must" when interpreting GDPR conditions, but the tier policy limits a T2 source to BEST_PRACTICE, not LEGAL. Two questions for the lead: should `normative_strength` follow the EDPB's own wording (MUST/SHOULD, as proposed here), or be capped, for example at SHOULD? And should a VIOLATION finding cite the GDPR LEGAL rule while treating the EDPB rule as the interpretive detail? The same pattern will recur for SRC-EDPB-DECEPTIVE and other regulator guidance, so an ADR may be warranted. (OPEN_QUESTION)
5. Prefix collision. `docs/rule-schema.md` §8 maps `EDPB` to SRC-EDPB-DECEPTIVE, and a prefix maps to exactly one source. Proposed decision:
   - register `EDPBCONSENT` → SRC-EDPB-CONSENT (example `EDPBCONSENT-86`);
   - before Phase 2 starts, rename the bare `EDPB` prefix to a document-specific one (for example `EDPBDDP` → SRC-EDPB-DECEPTIVE). The EDPB will probably contribute several documents (see the candidates above), and no EDPB rule IDs exist yet, so the rename breaks no published ID.

   Also confirm whether paragraph-number identifiers with a `-n` split suffix are acceptable under §3. This is a lead edit to `docs/rule-schema.md`, not made here. (OPEN_QUESTION)
6. Which cookies and trackers need consent is outside this document. Automated checks such as "nothing non-essential set before consent" (EDPBCONSENT-79-2, -90) need the ePrivacy Directive Art. 5(3) and EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 as sources; neither is registered or verified. (INCOMPLETE)
7. Relation to Opinion 08/2024 (consent or pay). The guidelines treat a genuinely equivalent service from the same controller as removing conditionality (para 37) and reject cookie walls (para 39). Opinion 08/2024 was not read this session, so how it refines paras 37–39 for paid alternatives is unknown. No contradiction is claimed. (OPEN_QUESTION; register the Opinion as a candidate source)
