## Scope and applicability

- Binding EU regulation, directly applicable in all member states (no transposition). Jurisdiction EU. Rule class LEGAL for rules derived from the articles.
- Material scope (Art. 2(1)): processing of personal data wholly or partly by automated means, and non-automated processing of data in or intended for a filing system. Art. 2(2)(c) excludes purely personal or household activity by a natural person.
- Territorial scope (Art. 3): controllers and processors established in the Union, wherever processing takes place (3(1)); and controllers or processors outside the Union that process personal data of data subjects who are in the Union when the processing relates to (a) offering goods or services to them, whether or not payment is required, or (b) monitoring their behaviour within the Union (3(2)).
- Binds controllers (Art. 4(7): the body that determines purposes and means of processing, alone or jointly) and processors. An audit can observe the controller's interface, not its processing records, so many obligations are only partly observable from UI.
- Platforms: all (web, PWA, iOS, Android, desktop, and non-digital collection). The Regulation is technology-neutral.
- Member-state variation exists within the text itself, for example the child-consent age in Art. 8(1) (16 by default; member states may set a lower age, not below 13). National derogations were not researched.
- Cookie and device-storage consent is governed by the ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC (not researched here); GDPR supplies the consent standard. The legal linkage between the two instruments was not read this session.

## Structure

- Title; citations; recitals (interpretive preamble, numbered in parentheses); enacting terms in chapters and articles. Relevant chapters read this session: Chapter I General provisions (Arts. 1–4), Chapter II Principles (Arts. 5–11), Chapter III Rights of the data subject (from Art. 12; Section 1 Transparency and modalities, Section 2 Information and access to personal data, Section 3 Rectification and erasure, Section 4 Right to object and automated individual decision-making, Section 5 Restrictions), Chapter IV Controller and processor (from Art. 24; Section 1 General obligations includes Art. 25). Later chapters and the total counts of recitals, chapters, and articles were not read (fetch truncation; see Uncertainties).
- Identifier scheme: Article N, paragraph (n), point (a); definitions are numbered points of Art. 4 (for example Art. 4(11) consent); recitals are numbered (1), (2), .... Rule IDs use prefix `GDPR` (`docs/rule-schema.md` §8): `GDPR-art-7-001`, `GDPR-art-4-11-001`, and `GDPR-rec-32-001` for recital-derived items.
- Normative vs informative: the articles are binding and use "shall" (MUST). Recitals state reasons and use "should"; they are not binding and serve as interpretive aids (INFORMATIVE). Some articles grant permissions ("may"), for example Art. 12(7) icons.

### Articles relevant to consent and privacy UI (paraphrased from the consolidated text)

- Art. 4(11) consent: any freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous indication of the data subject's wishes, given by a statement or by a "clear affirmative action", that signifies agreement to processing of personal data relating to them.
- Art. 5(1)(a): personal data are processed lawfully, fairly and in a transparent manner in relation to the data subject.
- Art. 6(1)(a): consent is one lawful basis, and it must be for one or more specific purposes.
- Art. 7 Conditions for consent, four paragraphs:
  - 7(1): where processing relies on consent, the controller must be able to demonstrate that the data subject consented.
  - 7(2): if consent is given in a written declaration that also concerns other matters, the consent request must be clearly distinguishable from those other matters, in an intelligible and easily accessible form, in clear and plain language. Parts of such a declaration that infringe the Regulation are not binding. This paragraph is conditional on the combined-declaration context; it is not a general form requirement for every consent request.
  - 7(3): the data subject may withdraw consent at any time; withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of earlier processing; the data subject must be told this before giving consent; "It shall be as easy to withdraw as to give consent."
  - 7(4): when assessing whether consent is freely given, utmost account is taken of whether performance of a contract (including provision of a service) is made conditional on consent to processing that is not necessary for that contract. This is an assessment factor, not a standalone prohibition.
  - Probe check: Art. 7 does not contain "pre-ticked" or "genuine or free choice"; those phrases are in Recitals 32 and 42. This confirms the discovery warning that summaries mix recitals into Art. 7.
- Art. 8: for information society services offered directly to a child, consent-based processing is lawful where the child is at least 16; below that, consent must be given or authorised by the holder of parental responsibility (8(1)); member states may lower the age to no less than 13; the controller must make reasonable efforts to verify parental consent, taking available technology into account (8(2)).
- Art. 9(2)(a): special categories of data may be processed on the basis of explicit consent (the word "explicit" is not further defined in the Article).
- Art. 12 Transparent information:
  - 12(1): information under Arts. 13 and 14 and communications under Arts. 15–22 and 34 must be concise, transparent, intelligible and easily accessible, in clear and plain language, in particular when addressed specifically to a child. It is provided in writing or by other means, including electronic means where appropriate. It may be given orally at the data subject's request if their identity is proven by other means.
  - 12(2): the controller must facilitate the exercise of data-subject rights (Arts. 15–22).
  - 12(3)–(4): the controller responds within one month, extendable by two further months, and must explain any refusal and the available remedies.
  - 12(5): information and actions are free of charge, except that a reasonable fee may be charged, or the request refused, when it is manifestly unfounded or excessive.
  - 12(6): the controller may request additional information to confirm identity.
  - 12(7): Art. 13–14 information may be combined with standardised icons; if presented electronically, the icons must be machine-readable.
  - 12(8): the Commission may adopt delegated acts on icons.
- Art. 13 Information collected from the data subject: provided "at the time when personal data are obtained".
  - 13(1)(a)–(f): controller identity and contact details (and representative); DPO contact details; purposes and legal basis; legitimate interests where Art. 6(1)(f) applies; recipients or categories of recipients; third-country transfers and safeguards.
  - 13(2)(a)–(f): storage period or the criteria for it; the rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection, and portability; the right to withdraw consent at any time where processing is based on Art. 6(1)(a) or 9(2)(a); the right to complain to a supervisory authority; whether providing the data is a statutory or contractual requirement, whether the data subject is obliged to provide it, and the consequences of not providing it; automated decision-making including profiling.
  - 13(3): information is due before further processing for a new purpose.
  - 13(4): the Article does not apply where the data subject already has the information.
- Art. 21(4): at the latest at the first communication, the right to object (21(1)–(2)) must be explicitly brought to the data subject's attention and presented clearly and separately from any other information. Art. 21(5): in the context of information society services, the data subject may exercise the right to object by automated means using technical specifications.
- Art. 25 Data protection by design and by default:
  - 25(1): the controller implements appropriate technical and organisational measures, such as pseudonymisation, that are designed to implement the data-protection principles, such as data minimisation. This applies both when the means of processing are determined and during processing, taking into account the state of the art, cost, and risk.
  - 25(2): by default only the personal data necessary for each specific purpose are processed. This covers the amount collected, the extent of processing, the storage period, and accessibility. By default, personal data must not be made accessible to an indefinite number of natural persons without the individual's intervention.
  - 25(3): approved certification may be used to demonstrate compliance.

### Recitals relevant to UI (non-binding, interpretive; read in the OJ text 32016R0679)

- Recital 32: consent by a clear affirmative act. Examples include ticking a box on a website and choosing technical settings. "Silence, pre-ticked boxes or inactivity" do not constitute consent. Consent covers all purposes when there are several. Electronic requests must be clear, concise, and not unnecessarily disruptive to use of the service.
- Recital 42: the controller should be able to demonstrate consent. Pre-formulated declarations should be intelligible, easily accessible, in clear and plain language, and without unfair terms. For consent to be informed, the data subject should know at least the controller's identity and the purposes. Consent is not freely given if there is no genuine or free choice or if the data subject cannot refuse or withdraw without detriment.
- Recital 43: consent is presumed not freely given if it does not allow separate consent for different processing operations where appropriate, or if performance of a contract or service depends on consent that is not necessary for it.
- Recital 58: transparency: information should be concise, easily accessible and easy to understand, in clear and plain language, plus visualisation where appropriate. It may be provided electronically, for example on a website. This is especially relevant where many actors and complex technology are involved, such as online advertising. Information addressed to a child should be in language the child can easily understand.
- Recital 60: standardised icons can give an easily visible, intelligible, and clearly legible overview of intended processing.
- Recital 78: examples of by-design and by-default measures are minimising processing, pseudonymising early, transparency about functions and processing, and letting data subjects monitor processing. Producers of products, services, and applications should be encouraged to consider data protection when developing and designing them.

## Cross-references

- ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC (not registered): the instrument that actually governs cookie and terminal-storage consent banners. It relies on the GDPR consent standard; the linkage was not verified this session. Candidate source.
- SRC-EDPB-CONSENT (candidate): EDPB interpretation of Arts. 4(11), 7, and 8 (granularity, withdrawal, cookie walls, scrolling). Needed before BP-consent-001 and GDPR-art-7-006 can be sharpened.
- SRC-EDPB-DECEPTIVE (candidate): deceptive design patterns interpreted against Art. 5(1)(a), Art. 12, and Art. 25. GDPR-art-5-001 and GDPR-art-25-001 are its likely `broader` anchors.
- SRC-EU-DSA (candidate): deceptive interface provisions for online platforms, which may overlap with consent UI.
- SRC-KR-PIPA (candidate): Korean counterpart for OTH-PRIVACY-UX and OTH-CONSENT-UX.
- Digital Omnibus (procedure 2025/0360/COD, COM(2025) 837): pending amendment to this Regulation and to Directive 2002/58/EC. T4 discovery pages claim it would move device-storage consent rules into new GDPR articles. That claim is unverified and is not used here.
- Accessibility sources (SRC-W3C-WCAG22, SRC-W3C-COGA): the "intelligible" and "clear and plain language" requirements overlap with cognitive-accessibility and readability rules (`overlaps`, not `equivalent`).
