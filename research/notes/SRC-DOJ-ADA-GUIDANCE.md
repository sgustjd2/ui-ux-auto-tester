## Scope and applicability

- DOJ's interpretive position that the ADA applies to web content: to state and local governments under Title II ("services, programs, or activities ... including those offered on the web") and to businesses open to the public under Title III ("goods, services, privileges, or activities ... including those offered on the web").
- It is guidance, not a regulation, and adopts no mandatory technical standard. It states that businesses and governments "can currently choose how they will ensure" online accessibility, and it references existing technical standards, including WCAG, as "helpful guidance" rather than requirements.
- Jurisdiction: US. This is interpretive guidance; the only codified technical standard on this track is the Title II rule.

## Candidate rules

- This source yields no NORMATIVE or LEGAL rules of its own with a specific technical threshold, because it mandates no standard. It supports the applicability statement that WCAG conformance is the DOJ-referenced way to reduce ADA web-accessibility risk for Title III, expressed as a `rule_class: BEST_PRACTICE` note (jurisdiction US) that points to the WCAG rules, not as a legal requirement.
- The binding US legal requirement is captured in SRC-DOJ-ADA-TITLE2 (Title II) and SRC-USAB-SECTION508 (federal agencies).

## Cross-references

- ADA Title II rule (SRC-DOJ-ADA-TITLE2): the codified requirement for state and local government; this guidance is distinct and, for those entities, superseded by the rule.
- WCAG (SRC-W3C-WCAG22): named as helpful guidance.

## Uncertainties and gaps

- Title III (private businesses) has no adopted federal technical standard; accessibility obligations are litigated case by case, with WCAG commonly used as the benchmark. Recorded as part of the GAP-007 resolution: ADA digital accessibility is codified only for Title II (WCAG 2.1 AA) and for federal agencies (Section 508, WCAG 2.0 AA); Title III relies on this guidance and case law.
