## Scope and applicability

- The national language institute's practical guide to writing public-sector Korean correctly and plainly. It targets public documents (drafts, press releases, reports, notices), not interfaces, but its notice-writing and plain-wording guidance applies directly to Korean interface copy on public services and is the government-level anchor for Korean plain-language microcopy that the Toss sources (T4, or T2 only on Apps in Toss) cannot provide.
- Jurisdiction: KR. Rule class BEST_PRACTICE (guidance). Whether a statute (for example the Framework Act on the Korean Language) obliges public bodies to follow it was not read and is not claimed.

## Structure (table of contents as printed on the page, paraphrased)

- Part 1, writing public language correctly: what public language is; how to write it correctly.
- Part 2, public documents in practice: draft documents, press releases, reports, notices (안내문).
- Appendices: index, a summary of the administrative work manual, 100 administrative terms to replace, 50 Japanese-style expressions to avoid, where to get help.

## Candidate rules

Families to extract in Phase 2 after reading the PDF (paraphrase only because of the no-derivatives license):

- Korean UI copy on public services avoids administrative jargon listed for replacement and uses the plainer alternative.
- Notices and instructions state the key point first and use plain sentence structures (to confirm against the notice chapter).
- Avoid Japanese-style expressions flagged by the guide.

Crosswalks: KRDS (SRC-KR-KRDS) for Korean public-service UI; Toss writing principles (SRC-TOSS-TECH-BLOG) and Apps in Toss writing rules (SRC-TOSS-APPS-IN-TOSS) as private-sector practice; GOV.UK content design (SRC-GOVUK-DESIGN-SYSTEM) as the English-language counterpart; WCAG 2.2 3.1.5 Reading Level (AAA) as the normative accessibility neighbour.
