## Scope and applicability

- A curated compilation of interaction and cognitive "laws" applied to UI design (`prd.md` §7.3 lists many of them). It is a T4 discovery index: it collects the laws, summarizes them, and (per law page) cites a primary source. It is not itself a rule source.
- Jurisdiction: GLOBAL. The laws are HEURISTIC/explanatory principles, never compliance requirements (`prd.md` §7.3).

## Structure

The site catalogues about 30 laws, including: Aesthetic-Usability Effect, Choice Overload, Chunking, Cognitive Bias, Cognitive Load, Doherty Threshold, Fitts's Law, Flow, Goal-Gradient Effect, Hick's Law, Jakob's Law, Law of Common Region, Law of Proximity, Law of Prägnanz, Law of Similarity, Law of Uniform Connectedness, Mental Model, Miller's Law, Occam's Razor, Paradox of the Active User, Pareto Principle, Parkinson's Law, Peak-End Rule, Postel's Law, Selective Attention, Serial Position Effect, Tesler's Law, Von Restorff Effect, Working Memory, and Zeigarnik Effect. The five "Law of ..." Gestalt entries correspond to the Gestalt principles in `prd.md` §7.3.

## Candidate rules

- The interaction laws become HEURISTIC rules that produce UX_RISK findings and explain observations; they are never VIOLATION sources. But per source-tier policy (`docs/standards-research-plan.md` §3), a T4 index cannot anchor a rule when a higher-tier source exists. Therefore:
  - Rules are anchored to the primary papers per law (T3 peer-reviewed research), which are registered as their own sources in a later action, using this index as the discovery map for the citations.
  - Some laws are also documented by NN/g (T3, SRC-NNG-ARTICLES) and by the Gestalt literature; those T3 sources anchor the rules where available.
- Because only this T4 index is verified so far, UX-INTERACTION-LAWS is PARTIAL. Registering the per-law primary papers is tracked as a gap.
